Anyone can argue these regulations. They are simply being put up now as general baseline rules and founding principles. As always, anything can be improved or swapped lightly, we're open to new ideas. 


CROS (cost recovery open source)

Developers and the financial management team will vote whether or not that project requires extra support (CROS). This will only need to happen if demand is greater than resources. If resources are greater than demand the project can stay public without heavy consideration.


Who votes

Devs, financial management (which could be backers) and end users. Each of those three vote in respective pools, The results from the finance pool, the dev pool, and the user pool can be seen in separate bars (or the default single bar), with extra considerations or notes available for viewing to get more in depth. Then, after everyone can see the other specialists ideas and sway, everyone goes to a final pool, which would just be everyone in one pool voting if the project should be CROS or start open.


If it ties

If the big pooled vote gets tied then there will be further consideration and more in depth opinion, being followed by yet another vote after that one with everyone, but this time more public facing promotion will go out to get more people in. If somehow that ties then the financial side decides on the best path moving forward.


How long it stays closed

This will be voted on for what makes sense for that specific project. There is a yearly re-vote until it opens. If a yearly re-vote has low turnout, it stays closed.


What is published

At very least and always, the cost for individual unit sourcing will be released to the public. For R&D, all Flamingo & Cactus finances are open to the public anyways. Other than those financials the specifics will need to be voted on with more specific project centralization.


After repayment

For products we sell direct, the price drops down, the calculation for the opened products pricing is: Fabrication + Components + Assembly + Shipping + Tax (on our side) = Raw cost, then Flamingo and Cactus group takes 1% of the raw cost as profit on every unit sold direct (we take an order from a customer and ship them a fully working product). We take 1% for ourselves on these direct orders only to ensure that we can handle unexpected difficulties, and maintain some financial backing for leverage of new projects and other (reasonable ventures). 


What can never be closed

Already open work is never closable.
