What the Flank Ledger is

The Flank Ledger is the running record of the Flamingo & Cactus group’s money: every deposit and withdrawal, the US dollar balance after each transaction, and the group’s physical silver holdings.

Where the numbers come from

The authoritative source is The Flank.md, a markdown file kept on the owner’s Desktop. This website is generated from it: scripts/build_flank.py reads that file and writes the ledger pages into site/flank/ — the ledger itself and this page — as part of the main site build. The ledger is a read-only viewer with nothing to log into and nothing to edit: when the ledger changes, the markdown is edited and the build is re-run. It is plain, dependency-free Python — no packages to install.

What the graph plots

The plotted figures are US dollar holdings only. There is one candlestick per transaction — hollow when the balance rose, solid when it fell — and the blue line is a moving average of the running balance over the ten calendar days ending on each transaction’s date. The window is counted in days rather than in transactions, so a busy week and a quiet one weigh the same. Silver is a physical reserve, tracked separately in grams and ounces in the ledger; it is not included in the plotted line, the moving average, or the headline totals. The ledger page shows an approximate dollar value for it under Silver Holdings, figured at the live spot price (troy ounces at XAG/USD) for reference. Where a candle jumps by more than the recorded deposit or withdrawal, its tooltip flags the difference as Unrecorded.

Where the live silver price comes from

The silver spot flag at the top of the ledger page and the approximate value under Silver Holdings are fetched live in the browser from the free public feed at api.gold-api.com (no key, XAG/USD per troy ounce), refreshed about once a minute. The flag also stamps the time of the last successful refresh, so it reads as alive even while the price sits on one cent. If the feed cannot be reached, the page shows the last price it saw and says so.

The rest of the page

Beneath the chart is the full ledger as a table, newest first. The search box filters by date, amount, or note text, and the dropdown narrows the list to deposits, withdrawals, or a single year. Everything on the site is plain static HTML — the logo and the ledger data are embedded directly in the page, so the ledger itself works offline and can be published as-is, for example from GitHub Pages. Only the live silver spot flag needs a connection; without one it shows the last price seen.

Why some transaction notes are blurred

Most of the ledger is public, but the notes on 26 transactions load blurred, because they name real people. A banner under the table says so, and the code box below it reveals those notes for that browser tab only — a fresh visit locks them again. This keeps casual visitors out, not someone reading the page source — the ledger data is still embedded in the page.
